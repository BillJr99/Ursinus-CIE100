In this first essay of 1200 to 1500 words, please construct an argument grounded in close reading of the texts we have discussed so far, which are Plato's Allegory of the Cave, Adichie's *Dear Ijeawele*, and Katie Merz's *Live the Questions*.  The essay takes up the first core question of the course, what should matter to me, by asking what it takes to see the world, and ourselves, more clearly.

## Prompt

Choose **one** of the following options.  Whichever option you choose, your essay must advance a specific, arguable thesis and support it with carefully selected quotations from the text(s), cited in MLA format.

1.  Plato and Adichie both describe a process of unlearning.  The prisoner has to turn away from the shadows, and Ijeawele's daughter has to be raised against the grain of the "conditioning" her culture supplies.  Construct an argument about what a real education requires, grounded in close reading of one or both texts.  You are welcome, though not required, to test your argument against an example of unlearning from your own experience or from contemporary life.

2.  The freed prisoner returns to the cave at real personal risk, and Adichie writes a letter she knows the very people it hopes to help may resist.  Construct an argument about what people who come to see differently owe the communities they came from, and about what limits, if any, bound that obligation.

3.  Katie Merz's *Live the Questions* poses its questions in glyphs and images instead of sentences.  Using the Smokestack installation alongside one written text from this unit, construct an argument about how visual and written texts pose questions differently, and about what each form can do that the other cannot.

## Scaffolded Deliverables

You will develop this essay in three stages, and together those stages make up the 100 points of this assignment.  The thesis statement, quote list, and rough outline are worth 10 points, the complete first draft is worth 30 points, and the revised final draft is worth 60 points.  You must meet **in person** with our Writing Fellow between the first and final drafts, and your final draft has to respond substantively to that conversation.  Cosmetic edits don't count as revision.  That meeting is worth **10 of the 100 points, all or nothing**, so meeting without revising earns zero on that row, and so does revising without meeting.  Please name the change the conference produced in your revision memo, so the credit is easy for me to see.  Book early, since the whole section shares one Writing Fellow.

1.  **Thesis Statement, Quote List, and Rough Outline.**  A one to two sentence working thesis, a list of four to six quotations (with page numbers) that you expect to use as evidence, and a paragraph-level outline.
2.  **First Draft.**  A complete draft of the full 1200 to 1500 words, and not a partial sketch.  The more complete your draft is, the more useful your Writing Fellow conference will be.
3.  **Final Draft.**  The revised essay, accompanied by a brief (one paragraph) revision memo describing what you changed and why.
